## 1. Regulatory framework (Ireland)

### 1.1 Specified Risk Material (SRM) — what can never be returned
- Ireland was recognised as having **negligible BSE risk** by Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/1321 (6 Aug 2021, OJ L 286, 10.8.2021), following OIE Resolution No 17 of 27 May 2021. **[V]** — <https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32021D1321>
- Reg. (EC) 999/2001 Annex V point 2: for animals originating in negligible-risk Member States, only the point 1(a)(i) tissues are SRM: **"the skull excluding the mandible and including the brain and eyes, and the spinal cord of animals aged over 12 months"**. **[V]** — <https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A02001R0999-20230101>
- Therefore **NOT SRM in Ireland**: the vertebral column (any age), dorsal root ganglia, tonsils, the last 4 m of small intestine, the caecum and the mesentery. **[V]**
  - **Consequence:** T-bone, porterhouse, bone-in sirloin/wing rib, côte de boeuf with the chine left on, and bone-in neck are all legal from cattle of **any age**, including cows over 30 months. A search result citing an FSAI info note confirms butchers no longer need to remove the vertebral column, with the change published in the OJ on 10 Aug 2021. **[S]** (the FSAI PDF itself returned 404)
  - The FSAI 2013 factsheet *Slaughter of Animals for Private Domestic Consumption* still lists the vertebral column over 30 months, and tonsils and intestines at all ages, as SRM. It is **superseded** on those points. **[V]**
- **Tongue:** the Annex V point 7 harvesting rule (a transverse cut rostral to the lingual process) **does not apply** to cattle from negligible-risk Member States. **[V]**
- **Head meat (cheeks) from cattle over 12 months** must be harvested at the slaughterhouse under a recognised control system (Annex V point 8). The frontal shot hole and foramen magnum must be sealed, heads with damaged eyes are excluded, and CNS testing applies. No exemption for negligible risk was found. Availability of cheek and head meat therefore **depends on whether the abattoir is set up for it**. **[V]** (rule) / **[?]** (per-abattoir practice)
- The **mandible** (jaw) is explicitly *not* SRM. **[V]**

### 1.2 Slaughter route
- **Approved slaughterhouse (DAFM or local-authority approved) — the normal route.** The meat can be eaten by the household. Sale requires a food business under the Hygiene Package. **[V]** (FSAI 2013 factsheet) — <https://fsai.ie/getmedia/3e9828a1-1179-4383-9e5b-a5ca384f2995/Slaughter-of-Animal-for-Private-Consumption-FINAL.pdf>
- **On-farm private domestic slaughter:**
  - It is legal for the household's own consumption only, and the meat "may not be supplied/sold to any third party".
  - Reg. 1099/2009 Art. 10 applies: stunning, competence and humane restraint.
  - The TSE rules still apply, so SRM must be removed.
  - The death must be notified to the District Veterinary Office (AIM).
  - ABPs must go to a rendering plant or knackery.
  - **[V]** (FSAI 2013)
- **App stance:** assume an approved abattoir and craft butcher, and mention the on-farm route in an info panel only.
- **Real Irish example (Teagasc, Mar 2023):** a Hereford × dairy heifer, 20 months old, 497 kg live → 245 kg carcass (49% kill-out). Killed at an Athenry abattoir and cut by a Ballinasloe butcher for **€330 to kill, butcher, bag and label**. Fifth quarter and offal were *not requested*. **[V]** — <https://teagasc.ie/publications/butchering-a-heifer-for-the-freezer-php/>
- **User's own experience of Irish abattoirs (2026-11-23):** offal and cheeks can **generally be returned**; hide return is decided **abattoir by abattoir**. **[S]** (user)
- Small Irish abattoirs and butchers generally treat the fifth quarter (hide, offal) as a **waste they must dispose of under ABP rules at a cost** unless the customer arranges otherwise. **[S]** (Gallagher's Butchers, Castlefinn, search excerpt). In factories, offal and hide are kept by the plant and factored into the price paid. **[S]** (Farmers Journal)

### 1.3 Chilling, cutting and mince (food business rules)
- Refrigeration of carcasses must begin directly after slaughter. Carcasses must reach **≤ 7 °C deep muscle** before boning, and cold or hot shortening must be avoided. **[V]** (Bord Bia MPQAS Processor Standard 3.13–3.14, reflecting Reg. 853/2004) — <https://www.bordbia.ie/globalassets/bordbia.ie/farmers--growers/farmers/qas/document-libraries/mpqas-pdfs/mpqas-processor.pdf>
- **Mince freshness:**
  - Mince must be made from fresh meat **< 6 days after slaughter, or < 15 days if boned and vacuum-packed**. **[V]** (MPQAS 3.15, following 853/2004 Annex III Sec. V)
  - Mince temperature must stay < 7 °C (ideally < 4 °C). Meat may be frozen for at most 18 months before mincing, and mince may only be frozen once. **[V]**
  - ⚠ **Implication:** in approved establishments, trim for mince is usually taken off early rather than after a long hang. Retail craft butchers supplying the final consumer fall largely outside 853/2004. **[?]** Confirm with the butcher how they handle mince from a 21–28 day hang.
- **Irish Grass Fed Beef PGI:**
  - Maturation must be at least **48 h, or 72 h for hindquarter cuts**. Carcasses with high pH (> 5.8) are excluded.
  - Eligible animals: steers and heifers ≤ 36 months (conformation better than O−, fat 2+ to 4+), and cows ≤ 120 months (conformation better than O+, fat 2+ to 5).
  - Mince must be ≥ 90% visual lean (VL).
  - Premium offals listed: cheek, tail, thick skirt, tongue.
  - **[S]** (Irish Food & Drink / Bord Bia page) — <https://www.irishfoodanddrink.com/irish-grass-fed-beef-pgi/>
- **Typical Irish craft-butcher hanging:** about 3 weeks for striploin per the Irish Butchers' Guild, with 21–28-day dry-aged products commonly sold (Tesco 28-day housekeeper's cut, James Whelan dry-aged). **[S]**
- **Quality Standard Mark (UK/AHDB, for comparison only):**
  - Livestock ≤ 30 months: at least 7 days' maturation for frying, roasting and grilling primals.
  - 30–36 months: at least 14 days (or hip suspension or electrical stimulation).
  - Bulls ≤ 16 months: at least 14 days.
  - **[V]** (AHDB Beef Yield Guide p.6)

### 1.4 Animal by-products (ABP) — Reg. 1069/2009, S.I. 187/2014
Verified against Reg. (EC) 1069/2009 (consolidated 14.12.2019) and Reg. (EU) 142/2011 (consolidated 29.10.2025). **[V]**
- **Category 1 (Art. 8):**
  - SRM.
  - TSE-suspect animals, *including their hides and skins*.
  - Animals given illegal treatment, or with residues above permitted levels.
- **Category 2 (Art. 9):**
  - Manure and **digestive tract content**.
  - Material declared unfit due to foreign bodies.
  - Animals that died other than by slaughter.
  - Foetuses.
  - Anything that is neither Category 1 nor Category 3.
- **Category 3 (Art. 10), the relevant items:**
  - (a) parts fit for human consumption but not intended for it for commercial reasons.
  - (b)(i) parts rejected as unfit but showing no sign of communicable disease.
  - **(b)(iii) "hides and skins, including trimmings and splitting thereof, horns and feet, including the phalanges and the carpus and metacarpus bones, tarsus and metatarsus bones"** of ruminants that are not subject to TSE testing, or that tested negative.
  - **(d) blood** of animals slaughtered after ante-mortem inspection (same TSE-test condition).
  - **(e) degreased bones, greaves.**
  - **(o) adipose tissue** from animals slaughtered in a slaughterhouse after ante-mortem inspection.
  - **Implication:** hide, horns, feet, blood, bones and fat are all **Category 3**, so they are not prohibited from being returned.
- **Hides (142/2011):**
  - "Untreated hides and skins" are tissue that has had no treatment other than cutting, chilling or freezing. They "may be placed on the market subject to the health conditions applicable to fresh meat" (Annex XIII Ch. V B.1).
  - "Treated" hides are dried, dry- or wet-salted for ≥ 14 days, and so on (Annex I def. 28).
  - **End point:** "hides and skins having undergone the complete process of tanning", wet blue, pickled pelts and limed hides "may be placed on the market without restrictions" (Annex XIII Ch. V C.2). **Once tanned, the hide is outside ABP controls.**
  - During transport, ABPs must be accompanied by a **commercial document** (Art. 17, Annex VIII Ch. III, produced in at least triplicate).
  - The competent authority may exempt operators handling **game trophies or other preparations for private or non-commercial purposes** from registration (Art. 20(4)(a)). Taxidermy is defined (Annex I def. 47).
  - **Practical route:**
    1. The abattoir releases the untreated hide, chilled or salted, with a commercial document.
    2. The hide goes to a tannery or taxidermist.
    3. The finished leather or hair-on hide comes back unrestricted.
    4. Whether the abattoir will do this is **abattoir-specific** (user).
- **ABP haulage:** ABPs can only be collected and transported by **DAFM-licensed hauliers**, with a Commercial Document. **[V]** (Irish ABP guide)
- **Source on market practice:** Irish hides are exported for tanning, and there's no domestic tannery. **[S]** (Farmers Journal)
- **Custom tanning:** single-hide custom tanning is rare in the UK and Ireland. US custom tanners charge about $175 per cow hide with a 12–14 month turnaround, and the hide must be fleshed and salted first. **[S]**

### 1.5 Organic (EU 2018/848 as implemented in Ireland)
- Ireland has two DAFM-approved control bodies: the **Irish Organic Association (IOA)** and the **Organic Trust**. **[S]** (Teagasc)
- *Organic Food and Farming Standards in Ireland*, Ed. 2.03 (2 Oct 2023) — <https://www.irishorganicassociation.ie/wp-content/uploads/Organic-Food-And-Farming-Standards-Ireland.pdf> **[V]**:
  - **1.04.40:** a subcontractor doing a preparation operation "**such as slaughtering, butchering**" must either **(a)** be certified in their own right, or **(b)** be listed on the operator's (farmer's) certificate and controlled as part of the operator's inspection. Either way, an organic farmer **can** use a non-certified abattoir or butcher *if it is arranged with the control body in advance* (1.04.41 application). Otherwise the meat **cannot be labelled or sold as organic**.
  - **3.04.40–41:** transport duration must be minimised, with **no electric or painful stimulation** during loading or unloading and **no allopathic tranquillisers** before or during transport.
  - **3.04.42:** records needed for animals leaving the holding: age, weight of slaughter animals, ID, date and destination.
  - **3.04.47:** suffering must be minimised "including at the time of slaughter".
  - **3.14.17:** only fit animals may be transported, clean and rested. Feeding hay 12 h before transport helps keep them clean.
  - **5.06.13 note:** organic carcasses must be **segregated in the chill**, ideally on a **separate designated rail**.
- In-conversion animals cannot be sold as organic. Veterinary withdrawal periods are **doubled**. Animals for meat may have at most one course of allopathic treatment per 12 months. **[V]** (Organic Trust FAQ) — <https://organictrust.ie/faqs>
- Labels: EU organic leaf logo, control body code, and the domestic body logo. **[S]**
- Meat for the household's **own consumption** isn't "placed on the market", so certification only matters if the meat (or a share of it) is to be **sold or described as organic**. **[E]** (reasoning from the scope of 2018/848, not stated outright)
- **The app should:** add an *organic* toggle that adds the cut-sheet instructions: certified or listed abattoir and butcher, separate rail, organic labelling, and keeping documents. Warn when sharing or selling.

## 2. Kill-out (dressing %) — live weight → carcass weight

### 2.0 What "carcass weight" means in Ireland **[V]**
- **Reg. 1308/2013 Annex IV A.I:** a "carcass" means "the whole body of a slaughtered animal as presented after bleeding, evisceration and skinning".
- **Reference presentation (A.IV):**
  - without the head (separated at the atlanto-occipital joint) and without the feet (severed at the carpometacarpal / tarsometatarsal joints);
  - without the thoracic and abdominal organs;
  - without the sexual organs and attached muscles, and without the udder or mammary fat.
- **Delegated Reg. 2017/1182 Art. 6(3):** bovine carcasses aged 8 months or more are presented **without kidneys, kidney fat, pelvic fat, thin skirt, thick skirt, the tail, the spinal cord, cod fat, fat on the inside of the topside, and the jugular vein and adjacent fat**.
  - Under Art. 6(4), Member States may allow partial removal of external fat from the haunch, sirloin, middle ribs, brisket point, ano-genital area and topside.
- **Weighing (2017/1182 Art. 7, Art. 14(3)):** the carcass is weighed warm, **within 60 minutes of sticking**. The **cold weight = warm weight − 2%**. Irish factory pricing is on cold weight.
- **Corrective factors (Impl. Reg. 2017/1184 Annex)** apply where a presentation differs. As % of carcass:

  | Item | Factor |
  |---|---|
  | Kidneys | 0.4 |
  | Kidney fat | 1.75 / 2.5 / 3.5 (fat 1–2 / 3 / 4–5) |
  | Pelvic fat | 0.5 |
  | Liver | 2.5 |
  | Thin skirt | 0.4 |
  | Thick skirt | 0.4 |
  | Tail | 0.4 |
  | Spinal cord | 0.05 |
  | Mammary fat | 1.0 |
  | Testicles | 0.3 |
  | Cod fat | 0.5 |
  | Inside-topside fat | 0.3 |
  | Jugular fat | 0.3 |
  | External fat removal | 0 / 0 / +2 / +3 / +4 (fat 1–5) |

- **Consequences for the app:**
  1. **Oxtail, thick skirt (onglet / hanger), thin skirt, kidneys, suet (kidney and pelvic fat) and cod or udder fat are not in the carcass weight.** They belong in the fifth-quarter list, and their weights come from live weight (AHDB) or from the corrective factors as % of carcass.
  2. A user-entered "carcass weight" from a factory or abattoir docket is the **cold weight**. Warm weight is cold ÷ 0.98.
  3. AHDB yields use the UK dressing spec, which is close to the EU reference. AHDB corrected EU-spec sides using the 1249/2008 coefficients, the predecessor of 2017/1184. Treat AHDB primal percentages as applying directly. **[E]**


See §2.0 for the carcass definition.

### 2.2 Breed-specific evidence
- **Jersey:**
  - In Teagasc project 5923, Jersey × HF males had a carcass of 277 kg vs 314 kg for HF and a **kill-out of 485 g/kg vs 495 g/kg (HF)**. The Jersey-sired animals were leaner, had poorer conformation (4.0 vs 4.7 on the 15-point scale) and a lower fat score (7.7 vs 8.5). **[V]** — <https://teagasc.ie/wp-content/uploads/media/website/publications/2015/Dairy_Beef_5923.pdf>
  - Bulls had higher kill-out, better conformation, greater m. longissimus area and less fat than steers. **[V]** (same)
  - Purebred Jersey cows had carcasses **75.8 kg lighter** than purebred HF cows, and 50% Jersey cows were 42.0 kg lighter. **[S]** (Teagasc Moorepark study, search excerpt)
  - The progeny of Jersey-cross dams had **yellower fat** and more fat scoring ≥ 5 for yellowness. **[S]**
  - Angus × (50% Jersey dam) steers were 13 kg lighter than Angus × HF steers, with similar conformation and fat. **[S]** (Farmers Journal 2019)
- **Angus:**
  - Irish steer carcass means: Angus 327.1 kg, HF 322.4 kg, Belgian Blue 363.2 kg. HF steers were slaughtered about 84 days older than Angus. **[S]** (EUROP study excerpt)
  - A Teagasc Angus heifer trial averaged 286 kg carcass at about O+ conformation and fat 4−. **[S]**
- **Hereford × dairy:** the 49.3% kill-out heifer above. **[V]**
- **AHDB reference:** a 600 kg R4L steer kills out at **53%**. **[V]** — AHDB Beef Yield Guide
- **2025 Irish national average carcass weights (DAFM via Agriland):** steers 346.5 kg, heifers 305.2 kg, young bulls 370.3 kg, **cows 298.6 kg**, bulls 447.8 kg. **[S]**
- **Irish cull cows (Minchin et al. 2009, Ir. J. Agric. Food Res. 48:75–86):**
  - Sample: n = 2,163 cows at an Irish factory in 2005.
  - Share meeting the target (cold carcass ≥ 272 kg, conformation ≥ P+, fat ≥ 3): **0.22 for Friesian-sired, 0.47 for early-maturing-beef-sired, 0.53 for late-maturing-beef-sired**.
  - **[V]** (abstract) — <https://research.ucc.ie/en/publications/prediction-of-cull-cow-carcass-characteristics-from-live-weight-a/>
- **ICBF Beef Breeds booklet (2013), Irish progeny averages** — cold carcass weight, grade, fat:
  - **Angus:** 306.7 kg, O+, 3+ (age 757 d; 60% from dairy dams).
  - **Hereford:** 310.5 kg, O+, 3+ (age 769 d; 62% from dairy dams).
  - "Cull Cow Weight (kg)": Angus 206.0, Hereford 206.7, Charolais 299.8, Limousin 261.3, Belgian Blue 260.0, Simmental 250.1, Shorthorn 207.0. The basis isn't stated; probably a carcass-weight genetic index or mean. **[S]** — <https://www.icbf.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/06/Beef_Expo_ICBF_Beef_Breeds_Booklet_sm.pdf>
- **Factors that drive kill-out:** meal feeding level, breeding, gender, age at slaughter, conformation (better grades kill out higher) and slaughter weight. **[S]** (DAFM PQ answer citing Teagasc)
- **Gut fill** affects live-weight-based kill-out (gut contents are 14.3% of live weight in the AHDB steer). The app should advise a live weight taken after a standard fast. **[E]**

### 3.2 Teagasc dissection (Conroy et al. 2010; McGee & Drennan, ASA 2016) — Irish cattle **[V]**
Source: <https://teagasc.ie/wp-content/uploads/media/website/publications/2016/ASA-Charleville-2016-Beef-Carcass-Classification-MMcG.pdf>

**Sample:**
- 662 animals: 115 bulls (13–17 months), 40 heifers (20 months), 507 steers (24 months).
- Four genotype groups: HF, early-maturing crosses, late-maturing crosses, and > 0.75 late-maturing.
- Full dissection: all visible fat removed and bones scraped.

**Steer means:**
- Slaughter weight 625 kg (435–884), cold carcass 333 kg (234–501).
- Conformation 6.8 and fat 8.5 (15-point scale).
- **Meat 679 g/kg (564–785), fat 123 (47–260), bone 197 (150–262).**

**Regression on the 15-point scales (intercept at conformation = fat = 8):**
- **Meat (g/kg)** = 698 (steer) / 704 (bull) / 713 (heifer) **+ 11.8 × (conf − 8) − 9.6 × (fat − 8)**, R² 0.73
- **Fat (g/kg)** = 113 / 96 / 100 **− 4.4 × (conf − 8) + 12.0 × (fat − 8)**, R² 0.67
- **Bone (g/kg)** = 190 / 200 / 187 **− 7.4 × (conf − 8) − 2.4 × (fat − 8)**, R² 0.71

**Other figures:**
- O=3= → R=3= (+3 conformation units): meat 66.5% → 69.9%.
- **HF steers (O−3+) 65.0% meat vs late-maturing suckler steers (U−3+) 71.2%.**
- 2015 Irish grade distribution:

  | | E | U | R | O | P |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Steers | – | 12 | 37 | 42 | 10 |
  | Heifers | – | 17 | 52 | 28 | 4 |

- **15-point scale:** E+ = 15 … P− = 1 for conformation, and 5+ = 15 … 1− = 1 for fat.

    | Day | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 14 | 21 |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
    | Loss % | 1.3 | 1.7 | 2.1 | 2.3 | 2.4 | 2.6 | 2.7 | 3.8 | 4.9 |

  - After day 3, sides lose about 0.1% per day. Irish carcass weight is a *cold* weight, so the first-day chill loss is already removed. **Model hanging loss on top of the cold weight as ≈ 0.1–0.12% per day.** **[E]**
- **AHDB R4H reference:** drip/cutting loss is 2.61% of carcass after 2 weeks' maturation. **[V]**
- **Wisconsin extension:** a carcass is 70–75% water and can lose 2–5% in the first 24 h. Worked example: 62% dressing, 3.5% cooler shrink, 67% cutting yield. **[S]**
- **Penn State:** retail yield falls steeply with yield grade. A 693 lb carcass yields 381/360/346/332/311 lb boneless for YG 1–5, and 533 lb bone-in at YG 2. **[S]** (partially summarised)
- **Irish home-kill reality check:** the Teagasc heifer gave **143.2 kg of meat from a 245 kg carcass = 58.4%**. That figure is after trimming, includes burgers, and keeps the T-bone and rib bone-in; it was O−4−. **[V]** This is about 7–10 points below the AHDB commercial yield for that grade. The plausible reasons are home-butcher fat trimming, bone left in, longer hanging and dairy-type seam fat. **[E]**
- **Irish seller rule of thumb:**
  - Hanging weight is 55–60% of live weight, and take-home is **55–65% of hanging weight**.
  - The cut split is about 25% steaks, 25% roasts and 50% mince or burgers.
  - "Choosing T-bones means sacrificing sirloin steaks."
  - **[S]** (Brave Herders, Munster)
- **Proposed model:**
  - Use AHDB Table 2 for the grade effect and the Teagasc regression as a cross-check.
  - Apply a **home-butchery adjustment** calibrated against the Teagasc heifer, plus a breed adjustment (HF −6 points vs late-maturing beef at equal grade per Teagasc; Jersey lower again).
  - Then apply the hanging loss.
  - Show results as a **range (±4–5%)** rather than a single number.
  - **[E]**

- **Implemented model (2026-11-23):**
  - **Level:** the absolute meat / fat / bone split comes from the AHDB 2010 grid (Table 2 saleable yield, Table 4 fat trim, bone & waste = the remainder), bilinearly interpolated on the 15-point scores. Hanging loss is taken off the meat; the cutting loss is part of the grid's bone & waste.
  - **Distribution:** the Beef Yield Guide (§4) is used only to share the meat out between regions and the lean-trim pool. Its own absolute level (about 77 % meat before drip at R4H, with ≤ 10 mm fat and 85 % VL trim) runs about 8 points above the grid and well above Irish home-kill results, so it is not used for the level.
  - **Trim pool** is 93 % VL, the grid's lean-trim definition. Mince at another lean % gains fat from, or sheds fat to, the fat pool.
  - **Primal / trim split check:** the grid counts flank as lean trim (about 23 % trim overall), while the Yield Guide lists thin flank and the short-rib plate as regions (about 14 % trim). Compared on the same basis, primals excluding flank are 75.8 % of meat in the Yield Guide and 76.4–77.3 % across every grid cell, so the Yield Guide distribution does not overstate steaks and roasts. A test (`engine.test.ts`) keeps the two within 2 points.
  - **Calibration (not validation):** the Teagasc heifer (O−4−, 245 kg, T-bone and rib on the bone; hanging time not stated, 21 days assumed) models at 165.8 kg (67.7 %) on the commercial basis against an actual 143.2 kg (58.4 %), a 9.2-point gap, consistent with the 7–10 points above. Per the user (2026-11-23), headline yields must reflect realistic expectations, so every cut weight and count is computed at the home-butcher level: commercial meat × 0.873 (8.5 of ~67 points). The difference is its own mass-balance term ("extra trim & waste"), not returnable fat. For this heifer the realistic level gives 144.8 kg. Because the factor is derived from this one animal, that match confirms the calibration rather than validating it.
  - **Why the gap? Not proven.** The source gives the cut list and totals only, not a breakdown of what was trimmed. Likely contributors:
    1. Harder trimming of fat, sinew and small lean scraps than in a commercial boning hall, where every scrap of 93 % VL trim is counted as saleable.
    2. Small lean trim kept by the butcher or discarded rather than minced.
    3. Seam fat typical of a dairy-cross heifer at fat 4−.
    4. Grade uncertainty: a whole fat class changes the grid yield by 2–3 points.
    5. Hanging time, if it was longer than the 21 days assumed.
    6. The cut list is rounded to whole kilograms.
    
    Bone left in is not a cause: the engine already counts the T-bone and rib bone. **Needed:** one or two more real Irish home-kill cut lists (ideally an Angus steer and a dairy cow) to test the 0.873 factor.

### 6.1 Mutually exclusive choices (practical)
- T-bone / porterhouse **vs** separate sirloin (striploin) + fillet steaks or roasts. **[S]** "Choosing T-bones means sacrificing sirloin steaks."
- Fore rib: on-the-bone roast **vs** côte de boeuf / tomahawk **vs** boned ribeye steaks or ribeye roast **vs** rolled rib.
- Wing rib (bone-in sirloin) **vs** boneless striploin.
- **Mixing within a cut (implemented 2026-11-23):** the fore rib is cut one rib at a time, so each of ribs 7–10 (4 per side, 8 per animal) can go a different way: on-the-bone roast (joints of whole ribs), rolled roast, côte de boeuf or tomahawk (one per rib, thickness = rib spacing, about 1½"), or boned for rib steaks. Any tomahawk rib still rules out bone-in short ribs. Topside, thick flank and the chuck eye are cut along their length, so they mix by number of joints, with the rest as steaks, diced or mince; the chuck is planned muscle by muscle. **[E]** (standard butchery practice)
- **Loin (implemented 2026-11-23):** per side, front to back: wing rib (ribs 11–13, sirloin only), short loin (6 lumbar vertebrae: sirloin above, fillet tail then centre below), fillet head (under the rump). T-bones are cut from the front of the short loin and porterhouses from the rear; the rest of the fillet can then come out. A Wellington or chateaubriand needs the centre fillet intact, so that side only gives front-section T-bones; a whole fillet needs a side with no T-bones. Different sides can be cut differently. The tail-under-the-front-third boundary is an anatomical estimate **[E]**.
- Rump: picanha off first (then rump steaks from the rest) **vs** whole rump steaks or roast. Tri-tip **vs** left in the rump or minced.
- Feather: feather blade (braise) **vs** flat iron steaks.
- Chuck & blade: housekeeper's cut roast **vs** braising steak **vs** diced **vs** mince. Denver steaks come out of the chuck roll.
- Brisket: whole or rolled **vs** spiced / salt **vs** mince.
- Topside, silverside and thick flank: roasts **vs** round / minute steaks **vs** diced **vs** mince. Eye of round can be split from the silverside.
- Plate / ribs: short ribs or Jacob's ladder **vs** mince.
- Shin: osso buco (bone-in) **vs** boneless shin **vs** mince.
- Thin flank: bavette + skirts **vs** all mince.
- Bones: return **vs** leave with the abattoir. Bone-in cuts reduce the bone available for marrow and stock bones.

### 10.8 Jersey cow kill-out: dedicated search (2026-11-23)

**Searched:**
- English: Irish, NZ, Australian, US, Danish and Brazilian literature.
- Danish (SEGES / LandbrugsAvisen) and Portuguese (SciELO) sources.
- USDA market reports and the NZ MPI review.

**Result: no measured Jersey cow kill-out exists in any source found.**

**Closest measured evidence:**
- **NZ cull dairy cows** (Muir et al., NZGA): DO% 43.9 / 42.4 / 41.4 % by year on unfasted on-farm live weight. The cows were 65 % Friesian, 27 % crossbred ("Kiwi") and 7.6 % Jersey, with no kill-out reported by breed. MPI's national NZ cull-cow figure is 42.6 %.
- **Irish cull cows** (Minchin 2009, n = 2,163, mostly Friesian): 46.4 %.
- **US cull cows** (J Anim Sci 2018 abstract; 734 Holstein, 1,234 Jersey):
  - Hot carcass: Holstein 285.2 kg, Jersey 193.7 kg.
  - Share at BCS ≥ 3: Holstein 21.4 %, Jersey 7.6 %.
  - 12th-rib fat: Holstein 0.27 cm, Jersey 0.21 cm.
  - Ribeye area: Holstein 47.0 cm², Jersey 44.1 cm².
  - No live weights reported.
- **Irish Jersey cow carcass** (Berry 2018): 204.3 kg vs 280.1 kg for Holstein-Friesian.
- **Non-cited calculator sites** quote 42–44 % and 45–52 % for Jersey. These were not used.

**Value used:**
- 43.8 %, derived as Irish cull-cow 46.4 % less the 2.6-point purebred Jersey vs Friesian offset (same-trial NZ steers).
- Consistent with the NZ measured cull dairy range of 41–44 %.
- Best way to firm it up: real Irish dockets for Jersey cows, with a mart or farm live weight and the factory carcass weight.
